How This Calculator Works

Formula Breakdown
Step 1: Calculate Silver Needed
Silver (grams) = (Target Concentration in g/L × Final Volume in mL) ÷ 1000

Example: For 500 mL at 150 g/L → (150 × 500) ÷ 1000 = 75 grams
Convert to troy oz: 75 g ÷ 31.1035 = 2.411 troy oz
Step 2: Calculate Nitric Acid Needed
Nitric Acid (mL) = Silver (grams) × 1.17 × (70 ÷ Your Acid %)

The baseline is 1.17 mL of 70% nitric per gram of silver. This ratio ensures complete dissolution with no free nitric remaining (saturated solution).

If using a different concentration, the amount is adjusted:
• 70% nitric: multiply by 1.0 (baseline)
• 50% nitric: multiply by 1.4 (need more volume)
• 90% nitric: multiply by 0.78 (need less volume)

Example: 75g silver with 70% nitric → 75 × 1.17 × 1.0 = 88 mL
Step 3: Calculate Distilled Water for Dissolution
Distilled Water for Dissolution = Nitric Acid (mL) × 1.4

The ratio is 1:1.4 (acid to water). This extra water accounts for evaporation during heating and provides better reaction conditions.

Example: 88 mL nitric × 1.4 = 123 mL Distilled Water

Saturated Solution Volume = Nitric + Distilled Water = 88 + 123 = 211 mL
Step 4: Calculate Additional Distilled Water for Final Volume
Additional Distilled Water = Final Volume - Saturated Solution Volume

After dissolving the silver, dilute the saturated solution to your target final volume.

Example: 500 mL - 211 mL = 289 mL additional distilled water

Total Distilled Water Used = 123 + 289 = 412 mL
Key Constants Used:
• 1 troy oz = 31.1035 grams
• 1.17 mL of 70% HNO₃ per gram Ag (saturated solution)
• 1:1.4 ratio of acid to distilled water for dissolution
• Concentration adjustment factor = 70 ÷ (your acid %)
Why These Ratios Matter:
The 1.17 mL per gram ratio creates a saturated solution with no free nitric acid. This is critical because free nitric will dissolve your silver anode during electrolysis instead of allowing proper plating. The electrolyte must be "saturated" (fully saturated) with silver to function correctly.
